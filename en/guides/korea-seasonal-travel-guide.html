Korea by Season: When to Go and What to Wear

Last updated: November 15, 2026 · About 6 min read

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文

Korea has four distinct seasons, so the scenery, clothing and things to enjoy change a great deal with the time of your trip. This article summarizes each season's features and travel tips. Weather differs greatly from year to year, so treat the periods and temperatures as rough tendencies and check the Korea Meteorological Administration forecast before you leave.

Spring (March to May): flowers and big temperature swings

In spring, flower scenery such as cherry blossoms and forsythia is popular. Cherry blossoms open first in the south, and in Seoul they usually peak from late March to early April, but the timing changes each year, so it is good to check the bloom forecast. The gap between daytime and night-time temperatures is large, so pack a thin jacket. In spring there are days of yellow dust or heavy fine dust, so some people prepare masks.

Summer (June to August): monsoon and heat

From late June into July is the rainy season, with frequent rain and high humidity. August often has continuing heat waves, so cut back on daytime outdoor plans and use indoor plans or early morning and evening. July and August are peak season for swimming, but beaches such as Haeundae get very crowded. When a typhoon passes, flights and ferries can be cancelled, so leave margin in your schedule. An umbrella, breathable clothing and sunscreen are essential.

Autumn (September to November): the best season for travel

Many clear, mild days make it the best season for traveling. For foliage, Seoraksan turns first in early to mid October, and Seoul's surroundings and downtown often peak from late October to early November. Foliage spots are very crowded on weekends, so I recommend weekdays or early hours. During the Chuseok holiday, transport is congested and some shops close.

Winter (December to February): cold and winter fun

In winter temperatures often drop below freezing, and in cold waves wind makes the felt temperature fall sharply. You need cold-weather items such as a thick coat, scarf, gloves and thermal underwear. In return there are winter pleasures such as snow scenery, ski resorts, hot springs and warm soups. Korean interiors are well heated, so layering clothes that are easy to put on and take off is good.

How to avoid holidays and festival periods

During the Seollal and Chuseok holidays, many people travel to their hometowns and the highways and trains are very crowded. Some restaurants and shops close, while on the other hand sights can be quieter than usual. If you travel in this period, book train tickets and accommodation early and check whether places are open.

Packing summary

  • Spring and autumn: a thin jacket, comfortable shoes, an umbrella
  • Summer: an umbrella and raincoat, breathable clothing, sunscreen, a portable fan
  • Winter: a warm coat, gloves, hand warmers, moisturizer
