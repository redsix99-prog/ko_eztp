Gwangjang Market Travel Guide: Getting There, Where to Eat and Local Tips

Last updated: November 25, 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文

About this place

One of Seoul's best-known traditional markets, said to have opened in 1905. Famous for bindaetteok, mayak gimbap and yukhoe; lines get long around lunchtime.

💡 Visiting tip

Stalls at the central crossroads can cost more than those on the edges, so compare price boards first. Most accept both cash and card.

Local tips

🚇 Getting there

It is about a 3-minute walk from Jongno 5-ga Stn (Line 1), Exits 7 and 8. The market also opens toward Cheonggyecheon for a pleasant walk.

🗺️ Suggested course

Bindaetteok, mayak gimbap and yukhoe at Gwangjang → Cheonggyecheon walk → walk up to Euljiro for a cafe → return from Jongno 3-ga.

☕ Cafe locals like

커피한약방 (Coffee Hanyakbang) — A cafe in an old herbal medicine shop in Euljiro 3-ga (about 15 minutes on foot from Gwangjang). Lunch lines are long, so go early. View on map ↗

Please check opening status and hours on Google Maps before you go.

Where to eat: menus & prices

  • Sunhee-ne Bindaetteok 순희네빈대떡 ↗
    Must-orderMung-bean pancake, meatballsPrice rangeAbout ₩6,000 per pancake, ~₩16,000 for a platter

    Stone-ground mung beans fried crisp right in front of you; a glass of makgeolli is the classic pairing.

  • Mayak gimbap stalls 마약김밥 골목 ↗
    Must-orderBite-size mini gimbap with mustard soy dipPrice range₩3,000–5,000 a plate

    Nicknamed “addictive” gimbap. Easy to eat standing or on the go, so it makes a good first bite.

  • Yukhoe (beef tartare) alley 육회 골목 ↗
    Must-orderYukhoe, yukhoe bibimbapPrice rangeAbout ₩20,000 a plate

    Yukhoe specialists line an alley on the east side: fresh raw beef with pear and egg yolk.

Prices are rough figures from online menus and reviews as of November 2026. They vary by shop and season, so check the menu on site.
