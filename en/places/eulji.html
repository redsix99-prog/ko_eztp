Euljiro alleys Travel Guide: Getting There, Where to Eat and Local Tips

Last updated: November 25, 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文

About this place

Old-school eateries and beer halls hide among printing shops and lighting stores, earning the nickname "Hipjiro". The faded signs and narrow lanes are a sight in themselves, and in the evening people gather at places like Nogari Alley.

💡 Visiting tip

Around Euljiro 3-ga Stn (Lines 2, 3). Print and lighting shops are working by day, so walk with care.

Local tips

🚇 Getting there

The shops cluster around Euljiro 3-ga Stn (Lines 2 and 3). There are many exits, so pick the one closest to your destination in a map app.

🗺️ Suggested course

Euljiro 3-ga Stn → printing-shop alleys and Nogari Alley → dessert at Coffee Hanyakbang → walk along Cheonggyecheon. The old alleys feel best in the evening.

☕ Cafe locals like

커피한약방 (Coffee Hanyakbang) — A cafe in an old herbal-medicine shop in Euljiro. The sign is small and it sits inside an alley, so use a map app. Some shops close on Sundays. View on map ↗

Please check opening status and hours on Google Maps before you go.

Where to eat: menus & prices

  • Euljiro nogari alley 을지로 노가리 골목 ↗
    Must-orderGrilled dried baby pollack with draft beerPrice rangeAbout ₩10,000 for a plate and a beer

    Near Euljiro 3-ga Stn, plastic stools fill a print-shop alley each evening for outdoor beer. Busiest 6–8 p.m.

  • Woo Lae Oak 우래옥 ↗
    Must-orderPyongyang-style cold buckwheat noodles, bulgogiPrice rangeCold noodles in the mid-to-high ₩10,000s

    One of Seoul’s defining Pyongyang naengmyeon houses, opened in 1946. The subtle buckwheat broth can surprise first-timers, but food lovers swear by it.

Prices are rough figures from online menus and reviews as of November 2026. They vary by shop and season, so check the menu on site.
