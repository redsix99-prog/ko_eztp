Busan Travel Guide: 3-Day Route, Local Food and Top Sights

Last updated: November 23, 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文

Gwangan Bridge at night — Doo-ho Kim, public domain

A port city of sea, night views and markets

Busan is Korea's second-largest city and its biggest port. City beaches like Haeundae and Gwangalli, brightly colored hillside villages and fish markets that open before dawn all share one city, giving it a very different mood from Seoul.

At a glance

  • From Seoul: About 2.5 hours by KTX (varies by train type)
  • Suggested stay: 3 days, 2 nights
  • Best time: May–June, Sept–Oct (beach season July–Aug)
  • Getting around: Subway and bus; the same transit card as Seoul works

Top sights in Busan

Gwangalli Beach

A beach facing Gwangan Bridge. The glowing bridge from dusk into the night is its biggest draw.

  • Getting there: From Gwangan Stn (Line 2), Exits 3 and 5, it is about a 10-minute walk to the beach. The road to the sea is straightforward.
  • Suggested course: Daytime beach walk → beachfront cafe at sunset → Gwangandaegyo Bridge night view → raw fish at Millak Waterside Park.
  • Where locals eat: 민락수변공원 회센터 (Millak Raw Fish Center) — Locals buy sashimi at the raw-fish center beside Millak Waterside Park and eat it on the steps. Prices vary by shop, so confirm weight and price first. View on map ↗

Haeundae Beach

Korea's best-known beach. Even outside summer, its promenade and skyscraper skyline draw crowds all year.

  • Getting there: From Haeundae Stn (Line 2), Exits 3 and 5, the beach is a 5–10 minute walk. The market is near the same station.
  • Suggested course: Haeundae Beach → Dongbaekseom trail and Nurimaru → Dalmaji-gil → Cheongsapo (check booking and operating sections for the Blueline Park beach train).
  • Where locals eat: 해운대시장 먹자골목 (Haeundae Market) — Haeundae Market near the station has street snacks, gukbap and seafood at local prices. Restaurants on the beachfront often charge tourist prices, so go one block inland. View on map ↗

Gamcheon Culture Village

Colorful houses climb the hillside in terraces. Murals and sculptures fill the alleys, so it is great for a slow walk and photos.

  • Getting there: Take village bus Seo-gu 2 from Toseong Stn (Line 1) (about 15 minutes), or Saha 1 from Goejeong Stn. Check bus numbers and timetables in a map app.
  • Suggested course: Alleys and murals → Little Prince photo spot → ride the same village bus back and have dinner at Jagalchi Market.
  • Where locals eat: 감천문화마을 골목 식당과 카페 — Alleys inside the village have cafes and restaurants with views. People live here, so walk quietly, and wear comfortable shoes for the slopes and stairs. View on map ↗

Jagalchi Market

Busan's signature fish market. You can eat raw fish and grilled seafood right in the market and enjoy the sea view.

  • Getting there: Exit 10 of Jagalchi Stn (Line 1) puts the market in sight. From Nampo Stn it is around a 5-minute walk.
  • Suggested course: Jagalchi Market → Gukje Market → Bupyeong Kkangtong night market → Yongdusan Park and Busan Tower, all on foot.
  • Where locals eat: 자갈치시장 2층 활어회 식당가 — The usual method is to choose fish from the tanks on the first floor and eat at a second-floor restaurant for a table-setting fee. Check the price list and fee first, and politely decline pushy touts. View on map ↗

Huinnyeoul Culture Village

A village on the cliffs of Yeongdo with alleys and small cafes along the sea. It is also known as a film location.

  • Getting there: From Nampo or Jagalchi Stn (Line 1), cross Yeongdo Bridge by bus or taxi to Huinnyeoul Culture Village in about 15 minutes. Check the bus number in a map app.
  • Suggested course: Huinnyeoul coastal path (about 1 hour) → a seaside cafe → back to Nampo-dong for street food at BIFF Square.
  • Where locals eat: BIFF광장 씨앗호떡 (Ssiat Hotteok) — Ssiat hotteok at BIFF Square in Nampo-dong is a Busan-style pancake filled with seeds and peanuts. People live in the Huinnyeoul alleys, so avoid loud voices and drones. View on map ↗

Taejongdae

A cliffside coastal park at the tip of Yeongdo. You can ride the circular train to the viewpoints and lighthouse.

  • Getting there: Buses such as 66 and 88 from Busan Stn or Nampo Stn (Line 1) reach Taejongdae in about 40 minutes. Check routes and timetables in a map app.
  • Suggested course: Entrance → Danubi shuttle train or walk to the observatory and lighthouse → seafood at Haenyeo Village → combine with Huinnyeoul Culture Village.
  • Where locals eat: 태종대 해녀촌 해산물 포장마차 (Taejongdae Haenyeo Village) — Stalls near the entrance serve seafood caught by haenyeo divers. Prices are often market price, so ask before ordering. View on map ↗

What to eat in Busan

Dwaeji-gukbap (pork soup with rice)

Busan's signature dish: sliced pork and rice in a milky broth simmered from pork bones for hours. Taste the broth first, then season it to your liking with salted shrimp, chive salad and dadaegi (chili paste).

  • Bonjeon Dwaeji-gukbap 본전돼지국밥 — About a 5-minute walk from Busan Station, handy right after you arrive by KTX. The broth is light rather than heavy, an easy start for first-timers.
  • Ssangdungi Dwaeji-gukbap (main branch) 쌍둥이돼지국밥 본점 — A local favorite in Daeyeon-dong, Nam-gu, known for its clean-tasting broth. The suyuk-baekban (boiled pork set) is the popular order.

Milmyeon (Busan cold noodles)

Busan-style cold noodles, born when Korean War refugees made noodles with wheat flour instead of buckwheat. Choose mul (icy broth) or bibim (spicy sauce), then add vinegar and mustard to taste.

  • Gaegeum Milmyeon 개금밀면 — A long-running milmyeon shop inside Gaegeum Alley Market, always on the list when locals talk about Busan milmyeon.
  • Chunhachudong Milmyeon 춘하추동밀면 — A popular milmyeon shop in Seomyeon, Busan's busiest downtown, easy to fit in between shopping or subway transfers.

Ssiat-hotteok (seed-filled pancake)

A Busan street snack: a pan-fried sweet pancake split open and packed with sunflower seeds, pumpkin seeds and peanuts. The sugar syrup inside is very hot, so hold it in its paper cup and let it cool a little.

  • BIFF Square hotteok stalls BIFF광장 씨앗호떡 거리 — Seed-hotteok stalls line BIFF Square in Nampo-dong, where the Busan International Film Festival began. It's a short walk from Jagalchi and Gukje markets.
  • Bupyeong Kkangtong Market 부평깡통시장 — A market with a night market in the evening, where you can try other Busan snacks like fish cakes and stuffed tofu pouches along with hotteok.

Busan eomuk (fish cake)

Busan's famous fish cake is chewy and rich because it contains a lot of fish. Eat it on skewers with hot broth at a street stall, or pick croquettes and fish-cake bars at a bakery-style shop.

  • Samjin Eomuk (Yeongdo flagship) 삼진어묵 영도 본점 — The flagship of a fish-cake maker founded in Yeongdo in 1953. Pick fish cakes onto a tray like at a bakery, and see a small exhibition on its history.
  • Goraesa Eomuk (Haeundae) 고래사어묵 해운대점 — A fish-cake brand with shops across Busan; the Haeundae store is handy for a snack or gifts after a beach walk.

Suggested itinerary

  1. Day 1: Gamcheon Culture Village → Jagalchi Market → Gukje & Kkangtong markets
  2. Day 2: Haeundae Beach walk → Gwangalli Beach and Gwangan Bridge at night
  3. Day 3: Huinnyeoul Culture Village → Taejongdae

Travel tips

  • Haeundae and Gwangalli are linked by subway Line 2, so you can see both in one day.
  • Gwangan Bridge lights up after dark; arrive around sunset to see both day and night.
  • Gamcheon and Huinnyeoul are residential villages, so please keep quiet.
  • Sea winds are often strong, so a light jacket is handy.

Hours, fees and closing days can change. Check the official site or a map app before you go.
