Seoraksan & Sokcho Travel Guide: 3-Day Route, Local Food and Top Sights

Last updated: November 23, 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文

Seoraksan — Christophe95, CC BY-SA 4.0

Land of the East Sea, rocky cliffs and autumn colors

Seoraksan, on Korea's east coast in Gangwon-do, is a national park famed for sharp rock peaks and deep valleys. Neighboring Sokcho is a port city of sea and mountains, so hiking, beach walks and market food all fit into a day or two.

At a glance

  • From Seoul: About 2.5–3 hours by express bus (depends on traffic)
  • Suggested stay: 2 days, 1 night
  • Best time: Autumn leaves in early to mid October; snowy winter scenery is popular too
  • Getting around: Sokcho city buses and taxis; bus to Seoraksan's entrance

Top sights in Seoraksan & Sokcho

Seoraksan National Park

Korea's signature national park, designated in 1970. Its highest peak, Daecheongbong, is 1,708 m, and its autumn colors are said to turn first in the country, so it is busiest in fall. A cable car or short trails already show its rocky scenery.

  • Getting there: An express bus from Seoul (Dong Seoul Terminal) to Sokcho takes about 2.5–3 hours. From Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal, city buses 7 and 7-1 reach Sorak Sopark in 30–40 minutes.
  • Suggested course: Sopark → Sinheungsa → cable car to Gwongeumseong → dinner in Sokcho. In the October foliage season, weekend traffic and parking are very crowded, so leave early.
  • Where locals eat: 설악산 입구 산채비빔밥·두부 식당가 — Restaurants serving sanchae bibimbap (mountain-vegetable bibimbap) and tofu dishes line the Sopark entrance, a warm meal after the mountain. View on map ↗

Ulsanbawi Rock

A giant rock peak of about 873 m that rings the edge of Seoraksan like a folding screen. Climb hundreds of steel steps to see Sokcho and the East Sea from the top. It takes time, so start early and check the weather.

  • Getting there: Take bus 7 or 7-1 from Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal to Sorak Sopark, then start on foot (about 3.8 km one way to Ulsanbawi).
  • Suggested course: Sopark → Sinheungsa → Heundeulbawi → Ulsanbawi summit. About 4–5 hours round trip, ending with a long steel staircase. Come down before dark.
  • Where locals eat: 속초관광수산시장 닭강정 (Chicken Gangjeong) — After the hike, buying dakgangjeong at Sokcho Tourist & Fishery Market is the classic course for locals and travelers alike. Always check the weather first. View on map ↗

Sinheungsa Temple & Heundeulbawi

At the entrance of Seoraksan stands Sinheungsa with its large bronze seated Buddha, the Unification Great Buddha. A bit further up is Heundeulbawi, a rock said to rock when pushed. The gentle slope suits a light walk.

  • Getting there: From Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal, buses 7 and 7-1 reach Sorak Sopark (30–40 minutes). From Sopark to Sinheungsa is a mostly flat 20–30 minute walk.
  • Suggested course: Sopark → Sinheungsa (Unification Buddha) → Heundeulbawi (1–1.5 hours round trip) → dinner in Sokcho. If you are short on energy, walking only to Sinheungsa is fine.
  • Where locals eat: 소공원 입구 산채비빔밥 식당가 — Sinheungsa is a temple over a thousand years old, so be quiet and dress modestly. Try sanchae bibimbap and acorn jelly at the restaurants at the entrance. View on map ↗

Naksansa Temple

A temple on a cliff facing the East Sea, famous for its seaside Haesu Gwaneum statue and Uisangdae pavilion. Restored after the big 2005 wildfire, it is also known as a sunrise spot.

  • Getting there: From Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal, a city or express bus toward Yangyang takes about 30 minutes to the Naksansa entrance. Check bus numbers and timetables in a map app.
  • Suggested course: Naksansa → Uisangdae → Hongnyeongam (hermitage on a rock) → stroll Naksan Beach → mulhoe at the restaurants.
  • Where locals eat: 낙산해변 물회 식당가 (Naksan Beach) — The temple overlooks the East Sea, so sunrise and sunset are especially good. Be quiet inside the grounds, and cool off with mulhoe at a Naksan Beach restaurant. View on map ↗

Abai Village & Gaetbae Ferry

A village settled by refugees from Hamgyeong Province during the Korean War. The gaetbae, a boat pulled along by hand on a steel cable across Cheongchoho Lake, is a Sokcho experience. You can also try Abai sundae, a local specialty.

  • Getting there: A taxi from Sokcho Intercity or Express Bus Terminal takes about 5–10 minutes, and city buses also run there. The Gaetbae ferry pier is the village entrance.
  • Suggested course: Sokcho Tourist & Fishery Market → Gaetbae (a pull-rope ferry) across Cheongchoho → Abai Village → sunset at Yeonggeumjeong → Sokcho Beach. All close to downtown.
  • Where locals eat: 아바이마을 아바이순대·오징어순대 식당가 — The village was built by North Korean refugees during the Korean War, and Abai sundae and squid sundae are famous. The Gaetbae ride itself is fun, so try it once. View on map ↗

Sokcho Tourist & Fish Market

Sokcho's biggest traditional market, famous for dakgangjeong, stuffed squid and fresh seafood. Lines at the dakgangjeong shops are often long, so allow plenty of time.

  • Getting there: Allow 5–10 minutes by city bus or taxi from Sokcho Intercity Bus Terminal. The market entrance in Jungang-dong is easy to spot from the main road.
  • Suggested course: Dakgangjeong and squid sundae at the market → Gaetbae to Abai Village → Yeonggeumjeong sunset → finish at Sokcho Beach. Half a day is enough.
  • Where locals eat: 만석닭강정 (Manseok Dakgangjeong) — Sokcho dakgangjeong is sweet-and-spicy Korean fried chicken, popular as a travel souvenir. The famous shops have long takeout lines, so go in the morning or late. View on map ↗

What to eat in Sokcho

Dakgangjeong (sweet-spicy fried chicken)

Crispy fried chicken tossed in a sweet-and-spicy glaze, the star of Sokcho Tourist & Fishery Market (Jungang Market). It's still good cold, so many people buy a box for the hotel or the bus home.

  • Manseok Dakgangjeong (market main shop) 만석닭강정 중앙시장 본점 — The shop that made Sokcho dakgangjeong famous nationwide; there's always a line at the main shop in the market. Choose mild or spicy.
  • Jungang Dakgangjeong 중앙닭강정 — Another popular dakgangjeong shop in the same market; many travelers buy from both to compare.

Abai sundae & squid sundae

Abai sundae, recreated by refugees from Hamgyeong Province, is a big sausage of pork intestine stuffed with glutinous rice, blood and vegetables. Squid sundae is a whole squid stuffed, steamed, then pan-fried in an egg coating.

  • Dancheon Sikdang 단천식당 — One of the oldest and best-known places on Abai Village's sundae alley; the set with sundae, soup and boiled pork is popular. Easy to visit after crossing on the gaetbae ferry.
  • Sindasin 신다신 — A sundae specialist inside Abai Village where you can try both abai sundae and squid sundae, nice with a glass of makgeolli.

Mulhoe & snow crab

Mulhoe is an East Coast favorite: fresh raw fish and vegetables in an icy, sweet-and-tangy broth, finished with noodles or rice as a meal. From winter to spring, snow crab and red crab are in season at harbor restaurants.

  • Bongpo Meoguri-jip 봉포머구리집 — A seaside restaurant that always comes up when people talk about Sokcho mulhoe, famous for mulhoe loaded with seafood. Expect long waits on summer weekends.
  • Cheongchosu Mulhoe (main) 청초수물회 본점 — A large mulhoe restaurant near Cheongchoho Lake with plenty of seating, convenient for families and groups.

Haksapyeong soft tofu

The soft-tofu village of Haksapyeong, on the way to Seoraksan, serves tofu made fresh every day. Eat the white tofu with seasoned soy sauce or as spicy sundubu stew. Many places open early, perfect before a hike.

  • Grandma Kim Young-ae's Sundubu (main) 김영애할머니순두부 본점 — Haksapyeong's best-known tofu house, opened in 1965. There's one set meal of sundubu with rice, served with bean-curd-residue stew and side dishes. Opens around 7 a.m.
  • Haksapyeong Tofu Village 학사평 순두부마을 — Several tofu restaurants share the same neighborhood, so if the famous one has a long wait, a neighbor will do just fine.

Suggested itinerary

  1. Day 1: Arrive in Sokcho → Sinheungsa & Heundeulbawi → Ulsanbawi or cable car
  2. Day 2: Abai Village & Gaetbae → Sokcho market → Naksansa

Travel tips

  • On autumn weekends the road to Seoraksan gets very crowded, so leave early in the morning.
  • Cable cars and some trails may be closed in strong wind or snow.
  • Ulsanbawi takes several hours round trip, so consider your stamina and sunset time.
  • Weather changes fast in the mountains; bring a jacket and water and wear hiking shoes.

Hours, fees and closing days can change. Check the official site or a map app before you go.
