Jeju Travel Guide: 3-Day Route, Local Food and Top Sights

Last updated: November 25, 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文

Aerial view of Seongsan Ilchulbong — Korea.net / Korean Culture and Information Service, CC BY-SA 2.0

A volcanic island of sea, forest and oreum

Jeju is a volcanic island built around Hallasan, with landscapes recognized as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site. Emerald seas, black basalt walls, oreum hills and forest trails sit close together, making it ideal for slow walks and rest.

At a glance

  • From Seoul: About 1 hr 10 min by plane from Gimpo Airport
  • Suggested stay: 4 days, 3 nights or more
  • Best time: Spring canola (Mar–Apr), autumn silver grass (Oct–Nov), winter camellias
  • Getting around: Rental car is easiest; buses and taxis also available

Top sights in Jeju

Seongsan Ilchulbong

A peak formed by a volcano that erupted under the sea about 5,000 years ago, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Climb the stairs to the top for the crater and sea views.

  • Getting there: It takes about 1 hour 20 minutes by bus east from Jeju Airport or Jeju City, or about 1 hour by rental car. Foreigners need an international driving permit for rental cars, so check in advance.
  • Suggested course: Climb Seongsan Ilchulbong (30–40 minutes) → ferry from Seongsan Port to Udo → Seopjikoji in the afternoon. For sunrise, arrive before the sunrise time.
  • Where locals eat: 성산일출봉 인근 해녀의집 (Haenyeo House) — At a haenyeo house in Seongsan you can try Jeju dishes like abalone porridge, sea urchin seaweed soup and mulhoe. Ingredients vary by season. View on map ↗

Hallasan

At 1,947 m, the highest mountain in South Korea. Some trails require advance reservations and the weather changes a lot, so check before you go.

  • Getting there: From Jeju Intercity Bus Terminal or Seogwipo, route buses on the 5·16 Road reach the Seongpanak trailhead in about 40 minutes. Check bus numbers and timetables in a map app.
  • Suggested course: Reaching the Baengnokdam summit requires advance booking, and the Seongpanak route takes about 9–10 hours round trip. If you cannot book, the Yeongsil route to Witseoreum takes 5–6 hours round trip.
  • Where locals eat: 삼대국수회관 (Samdae Guksu Hoegwan) — After the hike, rest over a bowl of gogi guksu (pork noodle soup) in Jeju City. A long-time local noodle shop; lunchtime queues can be long. View on map ↗

Saryeoni Forest Trail

A signature Jeju forest trail of towering cedars and broadleaf trees. Clean air and the scent of trees make it a "healing walk". Open sections and hours may change by season.

  • Getting there: A rental car from Jeju City (about 40 minutes) is the easiest way. Buses run on the 5·16 Road and Bijarim-ro routes, so check the timetable.
  • Suggested course: Walk the forest path from the Bulgeunoreum entrance (1.5–2 hours) → take a taxi from the other exit → Seongeup Folk Village. The two ends are far apart, so plan how to retrieve your car.
  • Where locals eat: 성읍민속마을 인근 식당가 (흑돼지·고사리해장국) — There are almost no restaurants near the trail, so bring food and water. Around Seongeup Folk Village you can try black pork and gosari haejang-guk. View on map ↗

Manjanggul Cave

A giant lava tube formed by volcanic activity, part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is cool even in midsummer, so bring a jacket; only part of the cave is open.

  • Getting there: About 40 minutes east of Jeju City by rental car (Gimnyeong, Gujwa). By bus, get off at Gimnyeong on the circular east-coast route and walk or take a taxi; check the timetable in a map app.
  • Suggested course: Manjanggul (about 1 km open, around 1 hour round trip) → Gimnyeong Beach → Woljeongri Beach → Sehwa Beach. It is cool inside the cave, so bring a jacket.
  • Where locals eat: 김녕·월정 해안 식당가 (성게미역국·해물라면) — The coastal restaurants in Gimnyeong and Woljeong serve sea urchin seaweed soup and seafood ramyeon. Beach cafes are good for a rest after the cave. View on map ↗

Udo Island

A small island reached by ferry from Seongsan Port. Ride a bike or e-scooter around the coast and enjoy snacks like peanut ice cream.

  • Getting there: The ferry from Seongsan Port or Jongdal Port takes about 15 minutes. Bring photo ID such as your passport, and check same-day sailings because strong winds can halt service.
  • Suggested course: Seongsan Port → ferry to Udo → loop the island by shuttle bus or e-bike (3–4 hours) → Seobin Baeksa and Udobong → return ferry.
  • Where locals eat: 우도 해물라면·땅콩 아이스크림 (Udo) — Udo's seafood ramyeon and peanut ice cream are the signature treats. Island restaurants fill up at lunch, so decide on a place soon after landing. View on map ↗

Yongmeori Coast

A coast beneath Sanbangsan where layered sandstone cliffs stretch into the sea like a dragon's head. Access may be restricted by waves and tides, so check first.

  • Getting there: By west-bound bus from Jeju City or Seogwipo, Sanbangsan stop takes about 1 hour 20–30 minutes. By rental car it is around 1 hour from Jeju City.
  • Suggested course: Yongmeori Coast (closed during high waves or high tide) → Sanbangsan → Songaksan trail → lunch at Moseulpo Port. A ferry to Marado is also available if you have time.
  • Where locals eat: 모슬포항 자리돔 물회·방어 식당가 — Entry hours depend on the tide, so check the day's notice. Around Moseulpo Port you can try jarigdom (damselfish) mulhoe and seasonal amberjack. View on map ↗

What to eat in Jeju

Jeju black pork BBQ

Thick cuts of Jeju black pork, skin on, grilled over charcoal. The texture is pleasantly chewy, and the Jeju way is to dip it in meljeot (anchovy sauce). Many places require an order of at least two portions.

  • Sukseongdo (Nohyeong main) 숙성도 노형본관 — A popular spot in Nohyeong-dong (New Jeju) known for aged black pork. Waits are long, so check the remote queue status before you go.
  • Jeju Black Pork Street 제주 흑돼지거리 — A street of black-pork BBQ restaurants near Dongmun Market and Tapdong in Jeju City. Compare menus and prices and pick the one you like.

Gogi-guksu (pork noodle soup)

A Jeju noodle soup: thick noodles in pork-bone broth topped with dombe-gogi (sliced boiled pork). If the rich broth sounds heavy, try the sweet-and-tangy bibim-guksu instead.

  • Jamae Guksu 자매국수 — Always on the list for Jeju pork noodles, with a short menu of gogi-guksu, bibim-guksu and dombe-gogi. A short drive from Jeju Airport.
  • Olle Guksu 올래국수 — A long-running gogi-guksu shop in Yeon-dong, Jeju City, with a clean broth and thick slices of pork. It closes early, mainly serving lunch, so go before noon.

Braised hairtail & sea urchin seaweed soup

Silver Jeju hairtail braised with radish in a spicy sauce, and seaweed soup made with sea urchin, are the classic tastes of Jeju's sea. The braised hairtail comes in a big pot, good for two or three to share.

  • Negeori Sikdang 네거리식당 — A long-running local restaurant in downtown Seogwipo; braised hairtail and clear hairtail soup are the signature dishes.
  • Sambo Sikdang 삼보식당 — A local restaurant near Seogwipo Maeil Olle Market, known for braised hairtail and abalone hot pot.

Hallabong & tangerines

Tangerines, hallabong and cheonhyehyang are in season from winter to early spring and are Jeju's go-to snack. Markets sell them singly or in small bags, and tangerine juice and ice cream are easy to find.

  • Dongmun Market 동문재래시장 — A big market in the middle of Jeju City with plenty of fruit stalls, plus a night market with street food in the evening.
  • Seogwipo Maeil Olle Market 서귀포매일올레시장 — A good stop when you're in the Seogwipo area, with tangerine and hallabong shops and snack stalls side by side.

Suggested itinerary

  1. Day 1: Arrive in Jeju City → Dongmun Market → Hyeopjae and Aewol coastal drive
  2. Day 2: Seongsan Ilchulbong → Udo Island loop
  3. Day 3: Saryeoni Forest Trail → Manjanggul Cave
  4. Day 4: Hallasan (check trail reservation) or Yongmeori Coast

Travel tips

  • Foreign visitors who want to rent a car need conditions such as an international driving permit, so check ahead.
  • Wind and fog can change or cancel flights, so leave some buffer on your return day.
  • Some Hallasan trails need advance reservations, and the weather changes a lot.
  • Only part of Manjanggul is open and the cave is cool. Access to Yongmeori Coast may be limited by tides.

Hours, fees and closing days can change. Check the official site or a map app before you go.
